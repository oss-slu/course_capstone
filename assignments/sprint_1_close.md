## Overview

This assignment documents your contribution to the project changelog for Sprint 1. Think of this as your formal record of what you shipped, why it matters, and what you learned—the kind of professional documentation that appears in release notes, portfolio pieces, and performance reviews.

**This is a firm deadline.** The SDLC doesn't pause for late submissions. Your work becomes part of the sprint record as submitted.

## What to Submit

Submit **two items**: one demo video and one written report.

---

### 1. Demo Video (Required)

Upload a short video (2–3 minutes preferred, 5 minutes maximum) demonstrating:

- Working functionality you implemented this sprint
- Clear explanation of the feature and its value to users
- Brief technical overview appropriate for a non-technical audience

This video should be suitable for sharing with clients or stakeholders. Present professionally.

**Submission options:**

- Direct upload to Canvas (MP4, MOV, WEBM, MKV, OGG)
- YouTube or Vimeo link (unlisted is fine)

Do not submit via Slack DM, email, or shared folder links.

---

### 2. Written Report (Required)

Submit a single document (PDF or MD) containing:

#### Pull Request List

List the URL(s) of pull requests you contributed to during this sprint.

- **Minimum:** 1 merged PR
- **Expected:** Multiple PRs demonstrating continuous momentum toward your team's milestone
- **Format:** URLs only, one per line

All supporting context—linked issues, acceptance criteria, design decisions, review discussions—should be documented in the PRs themselves.

#### Sprint Reflection (~500–750 words)

Address the following:

- **Delivery:** Evaluate the quality and completeness of your contributions
- **Collaboration:** Assess your effectiveness as a teammate and identify areas for improvement
- **Technical growth:** What new skills did you develop? What challenges did you overcome?
- **Professional development:** Reflect on communication, time management, and work habits

This reflection is for your professional growth. Grading verifies substantive content is present; the specific contents are not evaluated.

#### Work Log (Optional)

Include a log documenting your development activities: coding sessions, pairing, meetings, code review participation, discovery work, etc. Include dates, times, and neutral descriptions of activities.

Format is your choice. This helps contextualize your contributions and is especially useful when PRs don't fully capture the scope of your work.
