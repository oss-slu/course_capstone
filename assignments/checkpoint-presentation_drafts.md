## Overview

Your team will present a poster at the **SSE Undergraduate Showcase**. This is a public event where you'll demonstrate the value your product creates and showcase your team's work to faculty, students, industry guests, and potential stakeholders.

This checkpoint requires you to prepare your poster and presentation materials and conduct a trial run to identify areas for improvement before the showcase.

**Note for Graduate Students**: While this is administratively an undergraduate showcase (and Developers are responsible for this checkpoint), graduate students are welcome and encouraged to participate. This is a great opportunity to present your work to a broader audience.

## Presentation Style

Think of your poster presentation as similar to a **trade fair booth** rather than a traditional academic lecture. You should be prepared to:

- Give live demos of your product to visitors
- Engage in conversations with people who stop by
- Explain your work at different levels of detail depending on audience interest
- Answer questions about the technology, the problem you're solving, and your development process

**For research-oriented projects**: If your project has a strong research component, you may model your presentation on an academic poster session instead. Discuss with your Tech Lead and instructor which approach fits your project best.

## Context

Your poster presentation audience includes:

* Faculty and academic stakeholders
* Industry guests and potential employers
* Fellow students from across the School of Science and Engineering
* Your client (if attending)

Your goal is to clearly communicate:

* The value your product creates for users (not just what it does)
* The work your team accomplished this iteration
* Your development process and key technical decisions (explained for non-technical audiences)
* The future work needed to realize the product's full potential

## Requirements

### 1. Presentation Script with Speaker Notes ###

Create a detailed script that includes:

* **Speaker assignments:** Who presents which sections
* **Talking points:** Key messages for each section (not a word-for-word script, but comprehensive notes)
* **Transitions:** How you'll move between speakers and sections
* **Timing guidelines:** Approximate time allocation for each section
* **Backup plans:** How you'll handle questions or technical difficulties

Your presentation should cover:

* **Problem and Value Proposition:** What problem does your product solve? For whom? Why does it matter?
* **Product Overview:** What is your product? How does it work? (Demo or screenshots)
* **Work Accomplished:** What did your team build this iteration? What value does it deliver?
* **Development Process:** How did you work together? What key technical decisions did you make and why? (Explained for non-technical audiences)
* **Future Vision:** What work remains? What would another iteration enable?
* **Investment Case:** Why should stakeholders continue investing in this product?

### 2. Slide Deck ###

Create a visual presentation (PowerPoint, Google Slides, or similar) that includes:

* Clear, professional slides that support your narrative
* Screenshots showing your product in action
* Demo clips or screen recordings (if applicable)
* Minimal text per slide - visuals should support, not replace, your speaking
* Consistent formatting and professional design

## Recommended

### Trial Run and Feedback Documentation ###

Conduct at least one complete trial run of your presentation and document:

* **Recording:** Video of your trial run (doesn't need to be polished, but should be complete)
* **Self-assessment:** What worked well? What needs improvement?
* **Peer feedback:** Have team members or others watch and provide feedback
* **Improvement plan:** Specific changes you'll make before final delivery

## Deliverables

* Presentation script with speaker notes
* Slide deck with screenshots and demo clips
* Trial run video recording
* Feedback documentation and improvement plan

## Evaluation Criteria

**Substantive Engagement**

Evidence of thorough presentation preparation and practice, including:

* Complete, thoughtful presentation materials
* Serious trial run that tests the full presentation
* Meaningful feedback gathered and documented
* Clear plan for improvements based on practice

## Notes for Developers

* **This is practice:** The trial run is your chance to identify problems before they matter
* **Everyone presents:** All students on the team should have speaking roles
* **Think about your audience:** Board members and investors need different explanations than developers
* **Show, don't just tell:** Demos and screenshots make your work tangible
* **Practice together:** Coordination and transitions matter as much as individual sections
* **Time yourself:** Know if you're running short or long so you can adjust

## Notes for Tech Leads

* **You're coordinating:** Ensure the presentation tells a coherent story
* **Connect to strategy:** Your Product Strategy should inform the narrative
* **Balance technical and non-technical:** Technical decisions matter, but explain them accessibly
* **Think handoff:** This presentation may be how future teams learn about your work
* **Client preparation:** Consider sharing draft materials with your client for feedback
